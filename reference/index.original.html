RSI(7) divergence

Regular divergence between closes and RSI(7) on closed candles. Bearish: higher close high, lower RSI high. Bullish: lower close low, higher RSI low. Confirmed once two lower closes follow the second peak; forming means the second peak isn't confirmed yet; played out means RSI(7) has since reached the opposite zone (30 after a bearish one, 70 after a bullish one); failed means price closed beyond the second peak first. A status stays until one of those happens or the window ends (20 candles on 4H, 15 on 1D, 10 on 1W after the second peak). On USDT.D + USDC.D a bearish divergence points to a BTC low, so colours show what each one means for BTC: green BTC up, red BTC down, amber still forming.

How the estimates are made

  • RSI(7), Wilder smoothing on USDT.D + USDC.D, the sum of the two dominance closes on matching candles. BTCUSD (Coinbase) is loaded only to show how BTC behaved during past dominance swings and where it typically was at the target close; it does not set any swing.
  • Early warning from the timeframe below (added 14 Nov 2026). A higher-timeframe RSI(7) extreme almost never happens without the timeframe below getting there first (4H before 8H, 8H before 1D, 1D before 3D, 3D before 1W). When the lower one first closes at 70 (or 30) while the higher one is heading there, the card shows how often the higher one followed within 3 and 6 of its candles, next to the normal chance for any candle at that stage. In the 14 Nov study on USDT.D + USDC.D, 1D → 3D followed within 6 candles about 40% of the time against a normal 19%, and 3D → 1W about 85% against 40% (measured when the 1W used 60/40; with 70/30 it will follow less often). Most early warnings still don't lead to the higher extreme within a few candles, so treat them as raised odds, not a signal. The odds are recalculated from the saved history every time the page loads. 1H is not used: TradingView holds only about 74 days of hourly dominance data, and the page updates once a day.
  • Date accuracy on every card. Under each date the card shows how its date method did on past data: each past forecast used only swings that had finished by then, and the latest third was scored. In the 14 Nov study no method dated RSI extremes precisely: typically only 10–30% of dates fell within 25% of the actual wait. On the 3D card, a date built from the 15 most similar past 3D moments (RSI, how far the swing has run, and the daily RSI) beat the usual swing-length method (typical miss 13 vs 17 three-day candles, within 25%: 39% vs 23%), so the 3D card uses it whenever it still scores better on the saved history; the line under the date says which method is in use.
  • Weekly uses 30/70 (since 20 Nov 2026). The 1W cards track full swings between a close at or below 30 and a close at or above 70 (and back), the same as every other timeframe. These are cycle extremes and happen only a few times per cycle, so there are few past weekly swings: confirmed weekly dates will be rare and the tentative weekly box carries most of the weekly estimate.
  • Closed candles only. A zone counts when a candle closes with RSI(7) at or above 70, or at or below 30. The card says when RSI left the zone and came back within the same swing, rather than implying it stayed there. A candle counts as closed only if it had closed when you pressed Refresh, so a candle that was forming at refresh time is never treated as closed later. The swing starts at the first such close; further closes in the same zone don't restart it, and a new 30 only counts after a close at 70 (and the reverse).
  • Next-close level. Every card shows the exact close the next candle needs for RSI(7) to reach the target. This is calculated from the RSI formula, not estimated. When closed RSI is within 5 points of the target, this replaces the history-based date.
  • Dates name the candle by its open time, the way TradingView labels it. The page takes past swings in the same direction that already lasted longer than the current one, and uses the median of their remaining candles; the 25th–75th percentile gives the range.
  • Values at the target close. Two methods, chosen by a walk-forward backtest on 4H history back to January 2024 (USDT.D + USDC.D from January 2026) and daily history back to 2014–2018. Swing start: the median move of each ticker from start to end of past swings in the same direction, applied from its value at the start of the current swing. Updated for the move so far: once the swing is under way, the page picks the past swings that had moved about as much by the same stage (7–15 nearest) and applies their median remaining move to the value at the last closed candle. The 25th–75th percentile gives the range. Which one is used: 4H swings start with the first and switch to the second after one candle. Daily USDT.D + USDC.D does the same. Weekly is unchanged.
  • Measured accuracy (held-out swings, the latest third, never used for tuning). BTC level at the target close, typical miss: 4H 1.8% (no change 2.2%, previous method 1.9%); daily 5.9% when shown (no change 6.7%, previous method 7.5%). For USDT.D + USDC.D 4H swings once under way the miss fell from 1.3% to 0.7%. Four in five 4H misses were under about 4%, daily under about 14%. The range held the actual close about 55–60% of the time on 4H and about half the time on daily.
  • Divergence (RSI 7). Close pivots need 3 candles on the left and 2 on the right (2 and 2 on 1W); the two peaks must be 4–40 candles apart (3–30 on 1W) and the second within the last 20 candles on 4H, 15 on 1D and 10 on 1W. RSI must differ by more than 1 point. A divergence is played out when RSI(7) closes at the opposite zone before price closes beyond the second peak (that is a fail); if neither happens inside the window it expires. The history below uses exactly these rules on every past candle. Your rule is built in: the daily USDT.D + USDC.D status is the gate, and the 4H is only read once the daily shows a divergence, for a 4H divergence or RSI turning back from 70 (or 30). The weekly is context only.
  • BTC chips. How often BTC moved the expected way in past swings (green 65%+, amber 55–65%), BTC's own RSI(7) when those swings ended, and how far BTC typically ranged inside a swing.
  • Lacks confirmation rules. A card shows a date and levels only with 10 or more completed swings in that direction; a date also needs at least 5 past swings longer than the current one. A value row is withheld if that ticker moved the expected way in under 55% of past swings or if past swings disagree on direction. The exact next-close RSI trigger is always shown because it is maths, not history.
  • Weekly tentative forecast (1W card). Weekly history is short, so the confirmation rules above rarely allow a weekly date. This box gives one anyway, clearly marked as limited data. Step 1 is the next leg (USDT.D + USDC.D RSI(7) at the next 70 or 30, which has tended to line up with the next BTC low or high); step 2 is the turn after it. The estimate comes from past weekly USDT.D + USDC.D swings that already ran longer than the current one, blended with an RSI momentum estimate. Values are BTC's median move across those past swings. It is hidden when the card already has a confirmed weekly date, so a card never shows two dates for the same close.
  • Forecast history. Each card keeps a log of what it said, saved once per new closed candle: the closed RSI, the level it was heading for, the expected candle and range, and the exact next-close trigger. The Shift column compares each forecast with the one before it, so you can see the date sliding later or pulling earlier, or the swing flipping. Entries are saved only when every series loaded cleanly, and the log keeps the latest 120 candles per card. It starts from the first time the page is opened after this change; it is not rebuilt for earlier candles.
  • Accuracy from the backtest. Direction was right in roughly 56–72% of swings, depending on timeframe. Daily dates often missed by 20 or more candles, so use the range, not the single date. These are statistical estimates from past behaviour, not a forecast or financial advice.
